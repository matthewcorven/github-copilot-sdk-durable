2. Download time — 229.8 s for a full download, 266.9 s including the catalog round trip
===============================================================================

Method: the SDK's own telemetry line in logcat, which times the command it ran. Two downloads were
observed on this emulator, on different runs.

$ adb logcat -d | grep "ModelDownload Status"
11-25 11:54:18.131 I FoundryLocalCore: [INF] [Telemetry] AppName:BandEMobile UserAgent:FoundryLocalCore
  Command:ModelDownload Status:Success Direct:True Time:229813ms
11-25 12:10:29.922 I FoundryLocalCore: [INF] [Telemetry] AppName:BandEMobile UserAgent:FoundryLocalCore
  Command:ModelDownload Status:Success Direct:True Time:266876ms

229,813 ms = 229.8 s and 266,876 ms = 266.9 s. The difference between the two runs is the catalog
fetch and the retry behaviour around it, not the model.

The app's own wall-clock figure for the downloading run is NOT recorded here, and that is a gap
rather than an oversight to paper over: the harness only began writing its per-step trace after that
download, and logcat had rotated past it by the time the trace existed. What is recorded is the
screen at the end of the downloading run:

  model ready in 278.6 s · cache 837.4 MB (11 files, +837.4 MB this run)
  (screenshot: evidence/screenshots/e2-14-download-wall-clock-278s.png)

278.6 s of app wall clock against the SDK's 266.9 s of ModelDownload in the same run — the difference
is the catalog round trip and the engine's initialize. The honest summary is "about four and a half
minutes on this emulator, once". It is a one-time cost, and the reason the variant allows the network
for exactly this step and nothing else.

3. Memory — 603.7 MB process PSS of a 2,473 MB device
===============================================================================

Method: Debug.MemoryInfo.TotalPss for the app's own process, and ActivityManager.MemoryInfo for the
system, read by Chat/BridgeShim.cs (DeviceResources.ProcessPssMb / SystemMemoryMb) at the moment the
Measure button was pressed, with the model loaded and four turns committed.

$ adb shell cat .../e2-export/20261125T182918Z-measure/session.txt
## resources
captured_at: 2026-11-25T18:29:17.9035468+00:00
process_pss_mb: 603.7   (Debug.MemoryInfo.TotalPss / 1024)
system_mem_total_mb: 2473   (ActivityManager.MemoryInfo.TotalMem)
system_mem_available_mb: 777   (ActivityManager.MemoryInfo.AvailMem)
files_dir_free_bytes: 6707548160   (File.UsableSpace)
model_cache_bytes: 878065731   (11 files, directory walk)

PSS is the proportionally-shared resident set: the pages this process owns, plus its share of pages
shared with others. It is what the platform reports as "this app's memory", and it is the number to
compare against the device's total. 603.7 MB of 2,473 MB is 24% of this emulator's RAM for the app
with the model loaded, four turns committed, and 777 MB still available.

Readings from the same session, for the shape of the growth rather than a trend fit:

  612.8 MB — the per-turn reading taken as turn 4 committed (18:29:07 export)
  603.7 MB — the same process, measured 10 s later (18:29:17 measure, above)
  598.3 MB — an earlier build, four turns committed (18:00:18 measure)
  536/488  — earlier builds, one turn / none (17:57 / 17:11 exports)

The number is dominated by the model, not by the transcript: the store grew from 128,720 B to
133,801 B across the whole session, which is 5.1 KB against 604 MB. Both ends are the run's own
exports, not a pre-turn figure: 128,720 B is the turn-3 export before the kill
(evidence/e2-offline-demonstration.txt section 5) and 133,801 B is the turn-4 export after it
(section 8).

4. Generation speed — 0.5B model on this emulator
===============================================================================

Method: the SDK's own generated-token count for the turn (Generator.TokenCount in logcat) divided by
the SDK's own turn time (CoreChatCompletion Time in the same log). Both numbers are the SDK's, so
this is tokens/second in the sense the issue asks for, not a word count.

The demonstration run in evidence/e2-offline-demonstration.txt, whose four turns are the ones the
screenshots show. The pid column is included because turns 1-3 ran in one process and turn 4 in
another, after the kill:

$ adb logcat -d | grep -E "Generator.TokenCount|CoreChatCompletion Status" | grep -E "16359|16956"
11-25 13:23:58.215 16359  Generator.TokenCount -> Count:164
11-25 13:24:23.742 16359  Command:CoreChatCompletion Status:Success Direct:True Time:28776ms
11-25 13:25:13.414 16359  Generator.TokenCount -> Count:425
11-25 13:25:52.540 16359  Command:CoreChatCompletion Status:Success Direct:True Time:46803ms
11-25 13:26:18.283 16359  Generator.TokenCount -> Count:479
11-25 13:26:58.138 16359  Command:CoreChatCompletion Status:Success Direct:True Time:48590ms
11-25 13:28:26.758 16956  Generator.TokenCount -> Count:524
11-25 13:29:07.660 16956  Command:CoreChatCompletion Status:Success Direct:True Time:50606ms

turn 1 (2 messages):        164 tokens / 28.8 s =  5.7 tok/s
turn 2 (4 messages):        425 tokens / 46.8 s =  9.1 tok/s
turn 3 (6 messages):        479 tokens / 48.6 s =  9.9 tok/s
turn 4 (8 messages, new pid): 524 tokens / 50.6 s = 10.4 tok/s

An earlier build, whose turns were shorter, for comparison:

11-25 12:54:31.183  Count:133  / 12:54:56.044 Time:27566ms   =  4.8 tok/s
11-25 12:55:47.806  Count:394  / 12:56:12.482 Time:31170ms   = 12.6 tok/s
11-25 12:56:50.680  Count:426  / 12:57:17.359 Time:33428ms   = 12.7 tok/s
11-25 12:59:08.383  Count:484  / 12:59:36.544 Time:36030ms   = 13.4 tok/s

So the rate is **4.8-13.4 tok/s**, and it depends on the turn rather than being a property of the
device. Two effects are visible across both sets and neither is averaged away:

- **Turn 1 is always the slowest** (4.8 and 5.7 tok/s). The first turn after a model load does work
  the later ones do not.
- **Longer prompts are slower per token** (9.1-10.4 tok/s at 4-8 messages against 12.6-13.4 at 2-6).
  The prompt is re-processed every turn because there is no KV cache across calls: the SDK is
  stateless per request, and this is a cost of that.

For the README's table, the number to quote is the range **4.8-13.4 tok/s** with the reason, not a
single average that hides both effects.

Caveat, stated rather than buried: the upper bound on tokens here is 220 (the cap this variant sends
to keep a turn inside a budget; see section 6), so the counts above are the generated tokens, which is
below the cap because the model stopped early. The rate is measured on the same basis for all four
turns.

The app's on-screen figure is a word count over the reply plus the document ("words/s"), not tokens/s.
It is a different quantity — words are not tokens — and it is labelled on screen as words/s so nobody
reads two different measures as one. The tokens/s above is the one requirement 5 asks for.

That is the cost of on-device: 55.5 MiB of inference engine for arm64-v8a and 60.9 MiB for x86_64, summed from the embedded

engine lives in the service app — which is exactly the trade DECISION-bridge.md 6 records.

Deltas per turn, from the app's trace, are the streaming granularity a UI actually sees. The
demonstration's four turns:
  turn 1: 220 deltas for 1250 chars
  turn 2: 220 deltas for 1269 chars
  turn 3: 220 deltas for 1269 chars
  turn 4: 220 deltas for 1269 chars
220 is the cap, so those turns hit it. The earlier shorter turns in this run produced lower counts —
one delta per 5-6 characters either way — but those counts are **not quoted here as figures**. They
live in the demonstration run's `durable-store.jsonl`, which is app state and is deliberately not
committed (see evidence/e2-store-on-device.txt section 4); the committed evidence holds no export of
them. Quoting them would be quoting an artifact no reader of this repository can resolve, which is
the defect this file was corrected for, one level down.

===============================================================================
6. The generation cap, and why one exists
===============================================================================

The transport passes maxTokens=220. It is a cap, not a target. It exists because a 0.5B model
rewriting a ~1 200-character document generates past 200 tokens, and at 6-13 tok/s that is 30-50 s per
turn; without a cap an earlier attempt ran 45 s and 53 s with no natural end (logcat:
CoreChatCompletion Time:44982ms, 53514ms). Every turn of the demonstration is capped:

  turn 1  28.9 s      turn 2  46.9 s      turn 3  48.6 s      turn 4  50.9 s

(the app's own timer, one line per turn in evidence/e2-offline-demonstration.txt: the
`turn N: stream closed, …, NNNNN ms` lines at `:100`, `:108`, `:116` and `:246`; its
`elapsed_ms` exports say the same — `:161` (`28896`) and `:274`, `:165` (`46862`, one ms under the
line's `46863`) and `:278`, `:169` (`48601`) and `:282`, `:286` (`50867`). The SDK's own `CoreChatCompletion
Time` for the same four turns is the other clock and reads lower: 28.8 / 46.8 / 48.6 / 50.6 s,
`e2-resources.txt` section 4. The two sets are not interchangeable.)

The cap is a property of this demonstration, not of the layer: lib/Durable.cs is untouched by it and
the transport seam takes the value as an argument (IChatClient.CompleteStreamingAsync's maxTokens).
